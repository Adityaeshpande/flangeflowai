Inventory language

Abbreviations used in FlangeFlow

These terms describe planning records. They do not define whether a flange is technically suitable for a particular service.

SKU · Stock Keeping Unit
A unique code for one sellable flange configuration, such as its standard, size, pressure class, type and material.
FG · Finished Goods
Completed and accepted flanges available for sale or dispatch.
RM · Raw Material
Input metal or forging stock that will be converted into finished flanges.
BOM · Bill of Materials
The approved quantity and type of material required to manufacture one unit. In this demo, blank weight is a BOM assumption.
MOQ · Minimum Order Quantity
The smallest quantity a supplier will accept in one purchase order.
MTC / CMTR · Material Test Certificate or Certified Material Test Report
A supplier document reporting the material heat, specification and test results required by the purchase specification.
PO · Purchase Order
The formal commercial order issued to a supplier.
RFQ · Request for Quotation
A request asking candidate suppliers for price, lead time, compliance documents and commercial terms.
ROP · Reorder Point
The available-stock level at which purchasing should begin replenishment.
WIP · Work in Progress
Parts that have entered production but are not yet completed and accepted as finished goods.
Yield
The percentage of gross input material converted into planned usable output after normal process losses.
Lead time
The expected time from placing an order to receiving material ready for use.
Reserved stock
Inventory already committed to another order or production batch and therefore unavailable for a new plan.
Safety stock
A planned buffer held above expected demand to absorb normal uncertainty.
Heat number
An identifier linking material to a specific production batch and its test documentation for traceability.
NDT · Non-Destructive Testing
Inspection methods that examine a component without destroying it. The applicable method must come from the approved specification.

Flange language

What the product codes mean

These abbreviations help identify a product family. Exact dimensions, ratings and permitted materials must be checked in the applicable licensed standard and approved drawing.

NPS · Nominal Pipe Size
An inch-based nominal designation used for pipe and matching components. It is not always the actual measured outside diameter.
DN · Nominal Diameter
A metric nominal size designation commonly paired with PN-rated components.
Class
An ASME rating-class designation such as 150 or 300. It is not a direct pressure value by itself.
PN · Nominal Pressure
A pressure-rating designation used by some metric flange standards. Suitability still depends on material, temperature and the governing standard.
WN · Weld Neck
A flange type with a tapered hub intended for butt welding to pipe.
SO · Slip-On
A flange type that slips over the pipe and is joined by welding according to the approved procedure.
BL · Blind
A solid flange used to close the end of a piping connection.
A105
An ASTM carbon-steel forging specification used for piping applications. The complete purchase specification and current edition must be confirmed.
A182 F304 / F316
ASTM A182 stainless-steel forging grades. The grade must match the approved design and service requirements.
ASME
American Society of Mechanical Engineers, publisher of standards including ASME B16.5.
ASTM
ASTM International, publisher of material and test specifications including A105/A105M and A182/A182M.
ISO
International Organization for Standardization, publisher of international standards including ISO 7005-1.

Supplier guidance

Should we show vendors to contact?

Yes—but as a verified candidate directory, not a “recommended vendor” list. Suitability depends on the exact material form, grade, quantity, location, delivery date and quality plan.

Recommended product approach

Add supplier records with official contact details, supplied grades, approved status, lead time, MOQ, certificate status and past delivery performance. Keep “candidate,” “under qualification” and “approved” as separate statuses.

Technical and quality checks

  • Exact specification, grade and required material form
  • Current quality-management certification and scope
  • Heat-number traceability and required MTC/CMTR
  • Testing, inspection and subcontract-process controls
  • Sample or first-article approval before regular supply

Commercial and operating checks

  • MOQ, price basis and validity
  • Normal and expedited lead times
  • Capacity for the forecast volume
  • Packaging, transport and damage responsibility
  • Delivery, rejection and corrective-action performance

Important: Contact details and certifications can change. Verify them on the supplier’s official website and validate documents before adding any company to the approved vendor list. ISO now lists ISO 9001:2026 as the current quality-management standard, while existing certificates may be in a formal transition period.

Before contacting a supplier

Prepare a complete RFQ

A complete request reduces unsuitable quotations and prevents assumptions from becoming purchase requirements.

1. Item identity

Part number, description, approved drawing revision and intended material form.

2. Standards

Applicable dimensional, material, testing and documentation requirements with editions.

3. Quantity

Trial quantity, annual estimate, lot size and delivery schedule.

4. Traceability

Heat number, required certificate, marking and document-retention expectations.

5. Inspection

Required tests, inspection stage, third-party witness and acceptance criteria.

6. Commercial terms

Lead time, MOQ, price, freight, taxes, payment, rejection and replacement terms.
